What am I looking at?

Planet's Tanager-1 satellite doesn't take ordinary pictures. Every pixel it records is a 426-band spectrum from 377 to 2500 nm — closer to a fingerprint of what the ground is made of than to a colour photo. A normal browse image shows you about 15% of that.

You can normally only search this archive by date, place, and cloud cover. Fieldguide lets you search it by the spectra themselves:

  1. Pick a scene from the list on the left.
  2. Click a tile in that scene — each one covers 3.8 km of ground.
  3. Read the results: the tiles from anywhere else in the archive whose spectra look most like yours, with both curves plotted so you can see why.

Built on Planet's CC-BY Tanager open data. Amber dots mark tiles near published methane detections. Everything runs in your browser — no server, nothing uploaded.
